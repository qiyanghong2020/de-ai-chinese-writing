# English academic paragraph: preserve the evidence ceiling

**Case type:** English academic manuscript
**Source status:** Synthetic demonstration
**Goal:** Remove stock framing and repetition without converting an association into a causal claim.

## Input

> Importantly, it is worth noting that participants who received the reminder were observed to have a lower risk of missing follow-up visits. More specifically, the adjusted risk ratio was 0.74 (95% CI, 0.56–0.98) in the cohort of 218 participants. This finding highlights the important role that reminders may play in improving follow-up attendance. However, it should also be emphasized that, because the analysis was observational, the present results cannot establish a causal relationship. Taken together, these findings suggest that reminders represent a potentially valuable strategy that may contribute to better follow-up attendance.

## Protected facts

- cohort size: 218
- adjusted risk ratio: 0.74
- 95% confidence interval: 0.56–0.98
- observed direction: fewer missed follow-up visits with the reminder
- design: observational
- causal boundary: no causal conclusion

## Diagnosis

The paragraph states the same conclusion three times: lower observed risk, an “important role,” and a “potentially valuable strategy.” The first sentence and the estimate already carry the result. The rest should establish the design limit, not praise the finding again.

## Revision

> Among 218 participants, receipt of the reminder was associated with fewer missed follow-up visits (adjusted risk ratio, 0.74; 95% CI, 0.56–0.98). Because the analysis was observational, this association does not establish that the reminder caused the difference.

## Why this is safer and more natural

The revision keeps the estimate and its uncertainty beside the claim, removes duplicated interpretation, and ends on the study's actual limit. It does not add a mechanism, recommendation, or causal inference.
